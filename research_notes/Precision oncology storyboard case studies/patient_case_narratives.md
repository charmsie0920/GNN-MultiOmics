# Precision Oncology Patient Case Narratives for Storyboard Use

## Key Question 1: Documented individual patient stories from NCI-MATCH, IMPACT/COMPASS (MD Anderson), MSK-IMPACT, TAPUR, WIN Consortium, or similar programs where molecular profiling matched a patient to a targeted drug with a notable response

### Takeaway
The strongest, most fully-detailed **individual, named, citable patient case** found is Michael Wolff, a jazz musician treated at Memorial Sloan Kettering (MSK-IMPACT program), whose case was published as a formal case report in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (2018) and later covered by CBS News, Newsweek, and MSK's own media — making it an unusually well-triangulated, multi-source story. A second strong individual case is Dr. Lukas Wartman (Washington University in St. Louis genome sequencing program, not one of the four named trials but a landmark N-of-1 genomic-matching case), documented by a three-part *New York Times* investigative series. Cancer Commons (a patient advocacy nonprofit) also publishes many individual (first-name, de-identified) patient narratives, though most lack the specific mutation/drug/outcome detail needed for full citation-level rigor — flagged below. No single, fully detailed *named* individual patient narrative was found published specifically as a feature story from NCI-MATCH or TAPUR (only aggregate cohort statistics were found for those two trials); this is noted as a gap.

### Cited Findings — Individual Patient Cases (citable to a real source)

- **Michael Wolff (musician, MSK).** Diagnosed with what was initially thought to be lymphoma; comprehensive genomic sequencing (MSK-IMPACT program) revealed the true diagnosis was histiocytic sarcoma (a rare blood cancer, ~300 US cases/year) driven by an activating **MAP2K1 (MEK1)** mutation. He was given only ~2 months to live on standard therapy. Oncologist Dr. Mrinal Gounder treated him off-label with the MEK inhibitor **trametinib (brand name Mekinist)** — a drug developed for melanoma, not blood cancer. Tumor mass dropped ~80% within 10 days; he has remained cancer-free for roughly a decade, discontinued the drug, and returned to his music career. — [NEJM case report: Gounder MM, Solit DB, Tap WD. "Trametinib in Histiocytic Sarcoma with an Activating MAP2K1 (MEK1) Mutation." N Engl J Med. 2018;378(20):1945-1947.](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc1511490); [CBS News, "He had 2 months to live. Cancer research 'that seemed like science fiction' saved his life," April 26, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/cancer-genetic-sequencing-research-histiocytic-sarcoma-michael-wolff/); [MSK Giving, "A Second Chance at Life Thanks to Gene Sequencing at MSK"](https://giving.mskcc.org/impact/news/second-chance-at-life-thanks-to-gene-sequencing-at-msk); [MSK News, "Fixing One Wrong Note: How MSK Precision Diagnostics Saved a Musician With a Rare Cancer"](https://www.mskcc.org/news/how-msk-precision-diagnostics-saved-musician-with-rare); [Newsweek, "Are Cancer Cures Coming? Breakthroughs in Genetic Sequencing Show Positive Results," Jan 24, 2025](https://www.newsweek.com/2025/01/24/cancer-cures-genetic-sequencing-update-studies-scientists-2013421.html)
- **Dr. Lukas Wartman (Washington University in St. Louis, not MSK/NCI-MATCH/IMPACT/TAPUR but a foundational precision-oncology case).** A leukemia researcher himself diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL); after relapse in 2011, his own research colleagues sequenced his tumor's whole genome and, when DNA sequencing found no actionable target, **RNA sequencing** revealed the gene **FLT3 was massively overexpressed**. This could be targeted by **sunitinib (Sutent)** — a tyrosine kinase inhibitor approved only for kidney cancer, never before given for adult ALL. He became the first person ever treated with sunitinib for leukemia; his disease went into complete remission, he underwent a second (matched unrelated donor) stem cell transplant, and remained in remission as of the reporting. — [New York Times, Gina Kolata, "In Treatment for Leukemia, Glimpses of the Future" (part of the "Genetic Gamble" series), published online July 7, 2012 / print July 8, 2012](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/07/08/health/in-gene-sequencing-treatment-for-leukemia-glimpses-of-the-future.html); corroborating coverage: [Washington University School of Medicine, "Treatment for doctor with leukemia points to personalized medicine"](https://medicine.washu.edu/news/treatment-for-doctor-with-leukemia-points-to-personalized-medicine/); [Global Genes blog summary of NYT report](https://globalgenes.org/blog/dr-lukas-wartman-flt3-gene-causing-his-adult-acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia-is-stopped-with-sutent-reports-ny-times/)

### Cited Findings — Individual Patient Narratives with Partial/Limited Detail (advocacy-published, real but less independently verifiable)

- **Cancer Commons** (patient advocacy nonprofit) publishes a library of first-name, real (de-identified) patient stories on precision-matched treatment. Examples with at least partial mutation/drug/outcome detail:
  - **Rachel**, 52, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) with "numerous tumor masses and limited standard options" — connected via Cancer Commons to a clinical trial for a novel combination therapy plus adjuvant immunochemotherapy; achieved a **complete pathologic response** (no cancer found at surgery) and remains cancer-free. Specific drug names and exact biomarkers are not disclosed in the public story. — [Cancer Commons, "Complete Response: Rachel's Triple-Negative Breast Cancer Victory"](https://cancercommons.org/patients/rachel-breast)
  - **Elyse**, 55, metastatic endometrial cancer — treatments sequentially matched to shifting tumor biomarkers (DNA repair inhibitors, PI3K-pathway combination therapy, a CDK4/6 inhibitor), achieving sequential partial responses across 5+ therapy lines over multiple years. — [Cancer Commons summary](https://cancercommons.org/patient-stories/page/2/); [full story page](https://cancercommons.org/patients/elyse-endometrial)
  - **Susan**, 72, stage IV non-small cell lung cancer — genomic review by Cancer Commons found **ERBB2 alterations rather than EGFR mutations**, which prevented an oncologist from prescribing an incorrect (EGFR-targeted) treatment. Illustrates profiling's role in avoiding a *mismatched* therapy, not just finding a match. — [Cancer Commons, "Avoiding Costly Mismatches: Susan's Precision Treatment Story"](https://cancercommons.org/patients/susan-lung)
  - Other named Cancer Commons stories exist (Marta – pancreatic, KRAS G12D; Fiona – BRAF-fusion glioma; David – EGFR+ NSCLC; Ramona – triple-negative breast with rare mutation; Roberta – NSCLC complex mutations) listed at the patient-stories index but were not individually fetched for full detail in this research pass — [Cancer Commons Patient Stories index](https://cancercommons.org/patient-stories/page/2/)
  - **Caveat:** Cancer Commons stories are advocacy-organization content, not peer-reviewed case reports or independent journalism. They appear to be real patient accounts (the organization states it has guided >10,000 patients) but should be labeled to a pitch audience as "patient advocacy testimonial," not a clinical publication.

### Cited Findings — Aggregate/Cohort Data (NOT individual patient stories — do not present as single-patient narratives)

- **NCI-MATCH trial (aggregate only found):** Molecular profiling was completed successfully in 93.0% of 5,954 patient specimens; an actionable alteration was found in 37.6%; 17.8% were ultimately assigned to a matched treatment arm after exclusions. — [Journal of Clinical Oncology, "Molecular Landscape and Actionable Alterations in a Genomically Guided Cancer Clinical Trial: NCI-MATCH," ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.19.03010)
- **NCI-MATCH HER2 arm (aggregate, sub-cohort):** The trastuzumab-pertuzumab combination (approved for HER2+ breast cancer) shrank tumors in patients with HER2-amplified cancers of *other* histologies; confirmed overall response rate was 12% (3 of 25 evaluable patients), all three being partial responses. This is a small sub-cohort statistic, not a single traceable patient narrative — individual patients within this arm are not identified in the press materials found. — [ECOG-ACRIN press release, "NCI-MATCH cancer trial discovers a potentially broader role for an established dual HER2-blocking treatment"](https://ecog-acrin.org/new-discovery-from-nci-match-trial/); [PMC, "Tumor-specific activity of precision medicines in the NCI-MATCH trial"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10081392/)
- **MD Anderson IMPACT1 trial (Tsimberidou et al., aggregate only found):** Actionable genomic alterations were identified in 40% of 1,144 enrolled patients; among those, matched targeted therapy produced a response in 27% of patients vs. only 5% for unmatched treatment. Matched therapy was an independent predictor of longer overall survival. No individual patient case narrative was located in the search results — only cohort-level statistics from the published trial results and OncLive interview coverage. — [OncLive, "Dr. Tsimberidou on the IMPACT Trial in Patients With Advanced Cancer"](https://www.onclive.com/view/dr-tsimberidou-on-the-impact-trial-in-patients-with-advanced-cancer); [npj Precision Oncology, "Challenges and opportunities associated with the MD Anderson IMPACT2 randomized study"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41698-022-00317-0)
- **MSK-IMPACT cohort (aggregate):** Analysis of the first 10,336 patients sequenced via MSK-IMPACT found 11% were able to enroll in clinical trials of treatments directly targeting the genetic alterations found in their tumors. — [Newswise/MSK, "Landmark Analysis From Memorial Sloan Kettering Reveals Genomic Tumor Sequence of More Than 10,000 Cancer Patients Using MSK-IMPACT"](https://www.newswise.com/articles/landmark-analysis-from-memorial-sloan-kettering-reveals-genomic-tumor-sequence-of-more-than-10-000-cancer-patients-using-msk-impact)
- **TAPUR trial (aggregate):** Enrolled 3,000+ patients with advanced cancer across 272+ US sites; matches commercially available targeted drugs to genomic profiles off-label and tracks safety/efficacy outcomes at no cost to patients. No individual TAPUR patient narrative with full case detail was located in this search pass. — [ASCO, TAPUR Study overview](https://www.asco.org/research-data/tapur-study); [ASCO Post, "TAPUR Marks a Decade of Innovation"](https://ascopost.com/issues/june-25-2026/tapur-marks-a-decade-of-innovation/)
- **Maine Cancer Genomics Initiative (comparable program, aggregate):** Among 3,383 patients with advanced solid tumors, those with biomarkers matched to prospective-trial-evidenced therapy had median overall survival of 21.2 months vs. 12.8 months for patients on unmatched therapy. — [npj Precision Oncology, "Genome-matched treatments and patient outcomes in the Maine Cancer Genomics Initiative (MCGI)"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41698-024-00547-4); [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/05/240502141242.htm)

### Inferences
- The two fully-detailed, name-attached, multi-source-verified individual cases (Wolff, Wartman) both involve a dramatic narrative arc — misdiagnosis or a "no options left" prognosis, a sequencing breakthrough, an off-label repurposed drug, and a striking, quantifiable response (80% tumor shrinkage in 10 days; complete remission) — making them unusually strong candidates for a storyboard's emotional/narrative beats.
- Trial-sponsored programs (NCI-MATCH, TAPUR, IMPACT) publish rigorous aggregate statistics but rarely publicize single-patient narratives with full identifying/clinical detail in the sources indexed by general web search; their patient stories, if they exist, are more likely to be housed in trial-site patient testimonial pages, ASCO Daily News human-interest features, or the ClinicalTrials.gov results supplements — these need direct-site searching beyond what general web search surfaced (see Gaps).
- Cancer Commons functions as a patient-advocacy aggregator of many short real-patient narratives; these are useful for volume/breadth (a storyboard could show "many patients, many mutations") but each requires labeling as an advocacy testimonial rather than a peer-reviewed case report, since underlying data (specific gene, specific drug) is often withheld from the public-facing story.

### Gaps
- No specific, named individual patient story was found published directly by NCI-MATCH, TAPUR, or MD Anderson IMPACT/COMPASS as long-form journalism or a formal case report in this search pass; only cohort statistics. It's plausible such stories exist in ASCO Daily News, NCI's own "Cancer Currents" blog, or MD Anderson's "Cancerwise" patient-story blog, but these specific outlets were not directly searched/fetched due to tool-call budget — flagging for a follow-up researcher to check `cancerwise.mdanderson.org`, NCI's `cancer.gov` patient story features, and `dailynews.ascopubs.org` directly.
- WIN Consortium individual patient stories were not found in this search pass — no results returned for this program at all; a dedicated search of `winconsortium.org` was not completed within the tool budget.
- STAT News and NPR were specifically searched for a named precision-oncology patient profile; no specific matching article was surfaced (search returned only general precision-medicine coverage, not a STAT/NPR patient feature). This does not mean no such article exists — only that it wasn't found via the queries used.
- Full clinical detail (exact mutation, exact drug) for most of the other Cancer Commons stories listed (Marta/KRAS G12D pancreatic, Fiona/BRAF-fusion glioma, David/EGFR+ NSCLC, etc.) was not individually verified — only the index-page titles were captured, not the full story text.

## Key Question 2: Case reports in JCO Precision Oncology, Nature Medicine, Cell Reports Medicine, or similar journals describing a single patient's molecular-tumor-board journey

### Takeaway
JCO Precision Oncology runs a **dedicated recurring editorial series** — the "Molecular Tumor Board Case Discussion Series" — specifically built around single-patient case narratives with full molecular and treatment detail, making it the single best-fit citable journal source category for this pitch; the Michael Wolff case (Key Question 1) is itself an example of exactly this kind of single-patient case report, published in NEJM rather than JCO PO.

### Cited Findings
- JCO Precision Oncology launched a recurring **"Molecular Tumor Board (MTB) Case Discussion Series"** in 2018, explicitly designed so that "each MTB article will include a brief but comprehensive specific case discussion, which will cover a patient's treatment history as well as any molecular diagnostic test results," presenting the highlights of the actual tumor board discussion and any outcomes available. — [JCO Precision Oncology, "Introducing the JCO Precision Oncology Molecular Tumor Board Case Discussion Series" (2018)](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/PO.17.00310); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35135106/); [ASCO Connection announcement](https://connection.asco.org/magazine/society-member-news/jco-precision-oncology-launch-molecular-tumor-board-case-discussion)
- A specific recent installment in this series: **"Unveiling the Origins of Pathogenic TP53 Variants in Clinical Context: A Molecular Tumor Board Case Discussion"** — a single-patient case discussion of a TP53-variant tumor board decision. — [JCO Precision Oncology](https://ascopubs.org/doi/abs/10.1200/PO-25-00636)
- Separately, a cohort-level (not single-patient) MTB study found molecular tumor board review was associated with improved overall survival in non-small-cell lung cancer — useful as supporting aggregate evidence but not a patient narrative. — [JCO Precision Oncology, "Molecular Tumor Board Review and Improved Overall Survival in Non–Small-Cell Lung Cancer"](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/PO.21.00210)
- The Michael Wolff case (see Key Question 1) is itself a journal-published single-patient case report, appearing as a correspondence/letter in the *New England Journal of Medicine* rather than JCO PO. — [NEJM, Gounder et al. 2018](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc1511490)

### Inferences
- The JCO PO MTB series is a ready-made, ongoing library of exactly the kind of "single patient's diagnostic and treatment journey via molecular tumor board" content the pitch needs; a storyboard team should browse the series index directly on ascopubs.org rather than relying on individual search hits, since dozens of installments likely exist beyond the two identified here.

### Gaps
- This research pass surfaced the series and one specific 2025 example (TP53) but did not enumerate the full list of MTB Case Discussion Series installments, nor did it locate specific Nature Medicine or Cell Reports Medicine single-patient case reports (only JCO PO and NEJM examples were confirmed). A follow-up search directly on `ascopubs.org/journal/po` filtered to the MTB series, and on `nature.com/nm` / `cell.com/cell-reports-medicine` for "case report" precision oncology, would likely surface more examples but was not completed within the tool-call budget for this pass.

## Key Question 3: Long-form journalism profiling a specific real cancer patient who benefited from precision/genomic-matched therapy after standard treatment failed

### Takeaway
Two strong long-form journalism examples were confirmed: the *New York Times* "Genetic Gamble" series (2012, Gina Kolata) profiling Dr. Lukas Wartman, and CBS News' 2025 feature on Michael Wolff. A Newsweek 2019 cover feature on precision oncology broadly ("By Targeting Each Patient's Unique Tumor, Precision Medicine Is Crushing Once-Untreatable Cancers...") was also identified but not confirmed to profile one specific named patient in depth — flagged as needing direct-article verification.

### Cited Findings
- *New York Times*, Gina Kolata, three-part "Genetic Gamble" series, opening installment **"In Treatment for Leukemia, Glimpses of the Future,"** published July 7–8, 2012 — long-form profile of Dr. Lukas Wartman's genomic-sequencing-guided treatment (see full case detail in Key Question 1). — [NYT article](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/07/08/health/in-gene-sequencing-treatment-for-leukemia-glimpses-of-the-future.html); republished/summarized at [Harvard Medical School news](https://hms.harvard.edu/news/treatment-leukemia-glimpses-future) and [Longreads](https://longreads.com/2012/07/10/in-treatment-for-leukemia-glimpses-of-the-future/)
- CBS News, **"He had 2 months to live. Cancer research 'that seemed like science fiction' saved his life,"** published April 26, 2025 — long-form profile of Michael Wolff's histiocytic sarcoma diagnosis and trametinib treatment (see full detail in Key Question 1). — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/cancer-genetic-sequencing-research-histiocytic-sarcoma-michael-wolff/)
- Newsweek, **"Are Cancer Cures Coming? Breakthroughs in Genetic Sequencing Show Positive Results,"** published January 24, 2025 — features Michael Wolff's story within a broader precision-oncology overview. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2025/01/24/cancer-cures-genetic-sequencing-update-studies-scientists-2013421.html)
- Newsweek, **"By Targeting Each Patient's Unique Tumor, Precision Medicine Is Crushing Once-Untreatable Cancers. But Only a Fraction of Patients Currently Benefit. Can Medicine Close the Gap?"** published July 26, 2019 — title suggests patient-level narrative content but this pass did not fetch the full article to confirm a specific named patient case; flagged as a promising lead requiring direct fetch. — [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2019/07/26/targeting-each-patients-unique-tumor-precision-medicine-crushing-once-untreatable-cancers-1449287.html)
- CBS News also published a related but distinct rare-cancer-access story, **"Her son was dying, but his rare cancer made it difficult to get the right drug"** — this concerns insurance/access barriers to a genomically-matched drug rather than a trial-matched success story; the son's case was specifically noted as excluded from TAPUR eligibility because it was a primary brain tumor. This could be a useful "cautionary/access-barrier" counter-narrative for a pitch (showing the gap a computational tool might help close), though full clinical/mutation detail was not extracted in this pass. — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/rare-cancer-drugs-insurance/)

### Inferences
- Both confirmed long-form pieces (NYT/Wartman, CBS/Wolff) frame the story around the same structure useful for a pitch: standard treatment failure or misdiagnosis → genomic sequencing as a last resort → an unexpected, repurposed targeted drug → dramatic, quantifiable turnaround. This is a reusable narrative template for the storyboard even beyond these two specific citations.

### Gaps
- STAT News and NPR were searched directly but no specific named-patient precision-oncology feature article was surfaced in this pass (see Key Question 1 gaps) — a dedicated site-restricted search (`site:statnews.com` / `site:npr.org`) was not run due to tool budget and should be a follow-up.
- The 2019 Newsweek cover story was not fetched in full; whether it centers on one specific traceable patient (vs. composite/anonymized cases) is unconfirmed.

## Key Question 4: Patient advocacy organizations (My Cancer Genome, Cancer Commons, Friends of Cancer Research) publishing real patient stories about precision oncology

### Takeaway
**Cancer Commons** was confirmed to run an extensive, actively maintained library of real (first-name, de-identified) individual patient stories directly tied to genomic/biomarker-matched treatment decisions — this is the strongest advocacy-organization source found. **My Cancer Genome** and **Friends of Cancer Research** were not confirmed in this pass to publish comparable named patient-story content (no direct evidence found either way — true gap, not a negative finding).

### Cited Findings
- Cancer Commons states it has provided personalized cancer guidance to **over 10,000 patients**, matching tumor molecular profiles to therapies and monitoring for resistance pathways/next-step options. — [Cancer Commons homepage](https://www.cancercommons.org/)
- Cancer Commons' patient-stories index lists at least 21 individual named (first-name) patient stories spanning lung, pancreatic, breast (TNBC), glioblastoma, lymphoma, colorectal, and prostate cancers, each with a short synopsis of the precision-medicine angle (e.g., biomarker retesting, mismatch avoidance, CAR-T success, rare mutation discovery). — [Cancer Commons Patient Stories index](https://cancercommons.org/patient-stories/page/2/)
- Individual story detail confirmed by direct fetch: **Rachel** (TNBC, complete pathologic response via trial-matched combination therapy) — [link](https://cancercommons.org/patients/rachel-breast); **Susan** (NSCLC, ERBB2-vs-EGFR mismatch correction) and **Elyse** (metastatic endometrial cancer, sequential biomarker-matched therapies) — detail per Cancer Commons index summaries in Key Question 1 findings.

### Inferences
- Cancer Commons is best used in a storyboard as a *source of breadth* (many short, varied real patient vignettes covering different cancer types and different "profiling saved the day" scenarios) rather than depth — each story would need its own direct-page fetch for full clinical specificity before citing exact mutations/drugs in a pitch deck.

### Gaps
- My Cancer Genome (a Vanderbilt-affiliated precision oncology information site) was named in the assignment as a candidate source but was not directly searched/fetched in this pass — unknown whether it currently publishes named patient stories (it has historically been more of a mutation-reference database than a patient-story outlet, but this should be verified directly rather than assumed).
- Friends of Cancer Research was not directly searched/fetched in this pass; unknown whether it publishes individual patient narratives (its public content is more typically policy/advocacy-focused, but this is an assumption requiring direct verification).
- WIN Consortium's patient advocacy or patient-story content was not located at all in this research pass (see Key Question 1 gaps).
