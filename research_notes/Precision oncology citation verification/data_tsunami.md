# Citation verification: "data tsunami" / information-overload claim for the problem-statement slide

## Does the claimed paper exist, in this journal, in 2025, and can it be verified via DOI?

### Takeaway
The paper is real, but the title in the draft citation has one word wrong: it is "...bridging the **data deluge** to clinical decisions," not "data tsunami." It is genuinely in *Clinical and Experimental Medicine*, 2025, with a working DOI. "Data tsunami" is not this paper's phrase at all — it belongs to a different, separately real paper (ESMO Open, 2023), confirmed below.

### Cited Findings
- Exact, verified metadata for the Clinical and Experimental Medicine paper: title **"AI-driven multi-omics integration in precision oncology: bridging the data deluge to clinical decisions"**; journal **Clinical and Experimental Medicine**; authors Hsu CY, Askar S, Alshkarchy SS, Nayak PP, Attabi KAL, Khan MA, Mayan JA, Sharma MK, Islomov S, Soleimani Samarkhazan H; year **2025**; volume **26**, issue **1**; DOI **10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9&format=json&resultType=core); cross-checked against [Springer Link DOI resolver](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9) (same DOI, access-gated but the link itself resolves, i.e. the article page exists on Springer's site — it did not 404).
- Working open-access full text (PMC): **https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12634751/** (PMC12634751), confirmed to be this exact article via Europe PMC's full-text XML endpoint — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12634751/fullTextXML).
- Canonical DOI link: **https://doi.org/10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9**.
- The article is a review covering AI/ML integration of genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics and radiomics for cancer diagnosis, prognosis and treatment decisions (graph neural networks, transformers, explainable AI; therapy selection, early detection, non-invasive diagnostics; data harmonization, missing data, scalability, generalizability, regulation as challenges) — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9&format=json&resultType=core).
- Opening line of the abstract, verbatim: "Cancer's staggering molecular heterogeneity demands innovative approaches beyond traditional single-omics methods." — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9&format=json&resultType=core).

### Inferences
- The citation in the draft slide is almost certainly referring to this real paper, but the title has been garbled: "data tsunami" has been substituted for the paper's actual phrase, "data deluge." If the slide quotes the title verbatim with "data tsunami," that is a factual error in the title string and should be corrected to "data deluge" (or the citation should be re-pointed to the ESMO Open paper, which is the one that actually carries "tsunami" — see below).
- PubMed/PMC's own interactive pages (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) returned bot-check/CAPTCHA walls to automated fetches during this verification and could not be read directly; Europe PMC's REST API (api.ebi.ac.uk mirror, `ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest`) was used instead as an independent, non-publisher, non-PubMed source and returned consistent bibliographic metadata and full text for the same DOI/PMCID, which is strong independent corroboration that the record is genuine (not a hallucinated DOI).

### Gaps
- Did not get a page load of the Springer Clinical and Experimental Medicine journal's own article page past its login/IdP redirect (it redirected to `idp.springer.com/authorize...`), so the exact Springer-hosted abstract text was not independently re-confirmed on Springer's own rendered page; relied on Europe PMC's mirror of the same DOI/metadata and the open-access PMC copy instead. This is a minor gap — the DOI, title, journal and PMC full text all agree — but a reviewer who requires the Springer page itself specifically would need to open `https://doi.org/10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9` in an authenticated/non-bot browser.

## Does the paper contain language supporting the "information overload" / "clinicians overwhelmed by data" framing?

### Takeaway
Not in the specific worded form the user described ("clinicians may face an overload of information, increasing the complexity of decision-making"). That exact sentence, or a close paraphrase of it, does **not** appear anywhere in the paper's visible full text (introduction, the "data deluge" preprocessing section, discussion, conclusion, limitations, or abstract). The paper does discuss data volume as a technical/computational problem, but frames it as a problem for *biostatistics and computation*, not explicitly as clinician/oncologist information overload.

### Cited Findings
- Full-text search of the entire article (both halves of the Europe PMC full-text XML, covering introduction through references) for "overload," "complexity of decision," "complexity of decision-making," "informational burden," and "cognitive burden" returned **no matches** anywhere in the paper — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12634751/fullTextXML).
- The closest statement, from the Introduction, is about statistical/computational burden, not clinicians: "Volume overwhelms conventional biostatistics, as dimensionality (e.g., > 20,000 genes, > 500,000 CpG sites) dwarfs sample sizes in most cohorts" — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12634751/fullTextXML).
- "Data deluge" appears twice in the visible text: in the title itself, and as the heading of Section 4, "Overcoming the data deluge: preprocessing and quality control" — a section about batch correction, imputation and dimensionality reduction, not about clinician burden — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12634751/fullTextXML).
- The Conclusion frames data volume as something AI resolves into useful output, rather than describing clinicians as overwhelmed: "transforming the deluge of molecular, spatial, and clinical data into actionable insights for personalized cancer care" — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12634751/fullTextXML).
- A nearby passage, in a "Computational scalability and cost" subsection, addresses data-volume burden in terms of storage/compute, not clinician workload: "Whole-genome sequencing, high-resolution spatial proteomics, and serial radiomics can generate > 10 TB of raw data per patient" — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12634751/fullTextXML).

### Inferences
- The paper is thematically adjacent to the slide's claim (it is about the difficulty of handling huge, heterogeneous multi-omics datasets and the case for AI/computational integration), but it does not contain a direct statement that oncologists/clinicians personally face information overload or that manual interpretation (of gene expression, mutation panels, CNV, etc.) is overwhelming them. The "data deluge" in this paper is framed as a data-engineering/statistical-power problem (dimensionality, harmonization, storage, QC), not explicitly a clinician-cognition problem.
- Using this paper to support the specific "oncologists manually interpreting multi-omics face information overload" framing would be a stretch beyond what the text says; it would be more defensible to cite it only for the general "multi-omics data volume/heterogeneity requires AI-driven integration" claim, not for the clinician-overload framing specifically.

### Gaps
- The automated full-text search covered the visible/extracted full-text XML (introduction through references, retrieved in two sequential chunks covering the entire ~182,000-character document, confirmed to include the References section at the end). It is possible, though it was not found, that a differently-worded clinician-overload sentence exists in a figure caption, table, or box that didn't extract cleanly into the XML text stream; this residual risk is low given the full document was covered end-to-end.

## Is there a better/alternate source for the literal phrase "data tsunami," and is that paper real?

### Takeaway
Yes. **Aldea et al., "Precision medicine in the era of multi-omics: can the data tsunami guide rational treatment decision?", ESMO Open, 2023, vol. 8, issue 5, article 101642, DOI 10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.101642** is real, independently verified, and is the actual source of the phrase "data tsunami" — but note it is in the **title only**; the phrase itself does not recur in the article body. The body does, however, contain body-text language that is closer to the "clinicians overwhelmed by data volume" framing than the Clinical and Experimental Medicine paper does.

### Cited Findings
- Verified metadata: title "Precision medicine in the era of multi-omics: can the data tsunami guide rational treatment decision?"; journal **ESMO Open**; authors Aldea M, Friboulet L, Apcher S, Jaulin F, Mosele F, Sourisseau T, Soria JC, Nikolaev S, André F; year 2023; volume 8, issue 5; DOI **10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.101642** — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.101642&format=json&resultType=core).
- Open-access full text confirmed via PMC: **PMC10539962** — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10539962/fullTextXML); canonical DOI link **https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.101642** (resolves via redirect to `linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S2059702923008773`, confirming the publisher record exists under that DOI).
- "Data tsunami" appears **only in the title** (front matter); it is not repeated as a phrase in the abstract or body text — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10539962/fullTextXML).
- The body does contain closely related language about the volume of data being unmanageable by a single person, in Section 6.3 ("Persister cells"): **"The scientists and clinicians are however dealing with huge amount of data that cannot be integrated by a single person"** (sentence continues beyond what could be quoted in full, noting bioinformatics is reaching its limits for clinical decision support) — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10539962/fullTextXML).
- A related statement appears in Section 8.2 ("Merge information in a single device to model cancer biology"): **"It seems likely that only artificial intelligence approaches would be powerful enough to integrate massive data"** — [Europe PMC fullTextXML](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10539962/fullTextXML).
- Abstract opening sentence, verbatim: "Precision medicine for cancer is rapidly moving to an approach that integrates multiple dimensions of the biology." — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.101642&format=json&resultType=core).

### Inferences
- For a slide that wants to use the literal phrase "data tsunami" and attribute information-overload language to a source, **the ESMO Open (Aldea et al., 2023) paper is the correct and better citation for that phrase** — it is where "data tsunami" actually originates (in the title), and its body text ("huge amount of data that cannot be integrated by a single person") is closer in spirit to the "clinicians overwhelmed" framing than anything found in the Clinical and Experimental Medicine 2025 paper.
- However, even here, the exact sentence the user described ("clinicians may face an overload of information, increasing the complexity of decision-making") was not found verbatim in either paper. If the presentation needs that precise wording as a quotation, it should either be rephrased as a paraphrase attributed loosely to Aldea et al. 2023 (citing the Section 6.3 sentence above), or the search should be widened to other candidate sources, since neither of the two papers checked contains that exact sentence.

### Gaps
- Did not locate the specific verbatim sentence "clinicians may face an overload of information, increasing the complexity of decision-making" (or a close paraphrase) in either paper's full text. If this exact wording is required for a direct quotation on the slide, it is not supported by either source as currently cited, and a different/third source should be found for that specific sentence, or the slide text should be changed to a paraphrase that matches what Aldea et al. 2023 Section 6.3 actually says.
- The esmoopen.com and sciencedirect.com publisher pages themselves (as opposed to the Europe PMC/PMC mirror) returned bot-check pages during this verification and were not independently rendered; relied on Europe PMC's full-text mirror of the same PMCID/DOI instead, which is a secondary (though generally reliable, NIH/EBI-indexed) rather than primary-publisher rendering.

## Bottom-line recommendation for the slide

1. **The Clinical and Experimental Medicine paper is real** (Hsu et al. 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10238-025-01965-9, *Clinical and Experimental Medicine* vol. 26, issue 1), but its real title says **"data deluge,"** not "data tsunami." If the slide currently types the title with "data tsunami," that is a title transcription error and must be fixed to "bridging the data deluge to clinical decisions."
2. **This paper's body text does not contain a clinician-information-overload statement** in the specific form claimed (no "overload," no "complexity of decision-making" language anywhere in the full text, confirmed by an end-to-end search). Its "data deluge" language is about computational/statistical burden, not clinician cognitive burden. Citing it for the "data deluge" problem framing (volume/heterogeneity of omics data) is defensible; citing it for "oncologists personally face information overload" is not well supported by the text as written.
3. **The actual source of the phrase "data tsunami" is a different, separately real paper**: Aldea et al., *ESMO Open*, 2023, 8(5):101642, DOI 10.1016/j.esmoop.2023.101642. This paper is the better citation if the slide wants to keep the literal phrase "data tsunami," and its body (Section 6.3) has language closer to the overload framing ("huge amount of data that cannot be integrated by a single person... bioinformatics is reaching its limits").
4. Recommended fix for the slide: either (a) correct the Hsu et al. 2025 title to "data deluge" and drop the clinician-overload claim from that citation (cite it only for "multi-omics data volume/heterogeneity requires AI integration"), and separately cite Aldea et al. 2023 (*ESMO Open*) for the "data tsunami" phrase and the closer overload-adjacent language; or (b) cite Aldea et al. 2023 alone for both the phrase and the framing, since it is the stronger match on both counts.
