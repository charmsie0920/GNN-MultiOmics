## Does the paper actually report that 13 of 84 physicians (14.6%) answered all three VUS case vignettes with the literature-supported "most correct" answer? Does it also report 39/84 incorrectly defined a VUS, and over 75% recommended unwarranted genetic testing?

### Takeaway
All three statistics are confirmed as printed in the abstract **essentially verbatim** — including the 13/84 (14.6%) case-vignette figure, the 39/84 VUS-misdefinition figure, and the >75% unwarranted-testing-recommendation figure. One caveat worth flagging in the presentation: 13/84 is arithmetically 15.5%, not 14.6% — the 14.6% in the abstract itself appears to be an internal rounding/arithmetic inconsistency in the original published abstract, not an error introduced by whoever is citing it.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract sentence, quoted verbatim via Europe PMC's `abstractText` field: **"Only 13/84 (14.6%) responded to all three case examples with the answer deemed 'most correct' by review of literature."** — [Europe PMC REST API record (abstractText field)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10689-018-0086-2&format=json&resultType=core)
- Abstract sentence, quoted verbatim: **"Around half (39/84) of the physicians incorrectly defined a variant of uncertain significance (VUS)."** — [Europe PMC REST API record (abstractText field)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10689-018-0086-2&format=json&resultType=core)
- Abstract sentence, quoted verbatim: **"Over 75% made a recommendation for genetic testing that was not warranted."** — [Europe PMC REST API record (abstractText field)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10689-018-0086-2&format=json&resultType=core)
- NCBI's MEDLINE abstract record (independent retrieval path) corroborates the same three figures in paraphrase: "Only 13 of 84 respondents answered all three case examples in line with the published literature... About half of the 84 physicians analyzed defined a VUS incorrectly, and over 75% recommended genetic testing that was not warranted." — [NCBI E-utilities efetch (MEDLINE abstract)](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=29721668&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)
- Survey design/response details, corroborated by two independent sources: the survey was emailed to 488 physicians at Mayo Clinic Florida on August 16, 2017; 92 physicians responded; 84 respondents were analyzed for the three-case-vignette and VUS-definition statistics (the gap between 92 responders and 84 analyzed is not explained in the available abstract text). Physicians who specialized in cancer were significantly more likely to answer the vignettes appropriately (P = .02). — [Springer Medicine listing](https://www.springermedicine.com/physician-interpretation-of-variants-of-uncertain-significance/21608954), [NCBI E-utilities efetch (MEDLINE record)](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=29721668&rettype=medline&retmode=text)
- The paper's own conclusion, paraphrased consistently across sources: "a substantial proportion of physicians lack a true understanding of the implications [of] a VUS," and the authors recommend supplemental genetics training and greater awareness of/access to genetic counseling services. — [NCBI E-utilities efetch (MEDLINE abstract)](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=29721668&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)

### Inferences
- All three statistics in the citation to be checked are accurately attributed to this paper and can be cited as written: "13 of 84 physicians (14.6%)," "39 of 84 (nearly half) incorrectly defined a VUS," and "over 75% made an unwarranted genetic-testing recommendation."
- The "84–92 respondents" framing in the citation is accurate: 92 physicians responded to the survey overall, but the headline statistics (case-vignette accuracy, VUS-definition accuracy) are reported on a base of 84 analyzed respondents. If the presentation states a single denominator, 84 is the correct one to use for these specific percentages.
- The 14.6% figure, strictly as a percentage of 13/84, does not resolve arithmetically (13/84 = 15.5%, not 14.6%; 14.6% would correspond to roughly 13/89). This looks like an inconsistency inside the original published abstract itself (not something the presentation's drafter introduced), so it should either be reported exactly as the paper states it ("13/84 (14.6%)," trusting the published abstract's own phrasing) or flagged with a footnote noting the apparent rounding/arithmetic discrepancy. It is not evidence the figure is misattributed to the wrong paper — the sentence appears verbatim in this paper's abstract.
